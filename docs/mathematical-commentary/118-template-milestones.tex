\section{Five further completed template families}
\label{sec:copying-four-templates}
This development completes contraction associativity, contraction
commutativity, garbage collection and pulling weakening or contraction through a box.
For each family the pre-template is defined from the literal graph pair,
with independent recursive graph isomorphisms on its two sides. Output
closure, input safety and both robustness directions are proved for the
original copying machine. The resulting contextual equivalences preserve
\emph{every exact successful transition count}, in arbitrary original
focus-free observing contexts, including addressed sites under boxes.
The copying and parametricity families remain unfinished.
The unchanged terms of Proposition~12.2 and their Figure~16 translation
are not replaced by simpler programs.

\subsection{Garbage collection}
\termdef{literal-garbage-collection}
Let $C:[]\Rightarrow\mathsf{term}$ be any finite acyclic term-sharing tree,
and let $H:\mathsf{term}\Rightarrow\mathsf{term}^{k}\mathsf{atom}^{h}$ be
an original copyable, focus-free payload. The garbage-collection pair is
\[
 C;H\quad\sim\quad D^{\mathsf{term}}_{k,0}\otimes D^{\mathsf{atom}}_{h,0}.
\]
The right side is the original ordered zero-use distributor, including its
actual weakening edges. The left side retains the entire zero-input tree
and the entire payload, including any recursively boxed lambda body.
Copyability is an explicit premise of the family, as required by the
paper's use of $H$ in Figure~21.

The primitive-label lemmas prove focus freedom by serial and parallel
assembly. Inspection of the literal constructor proves that its input list
is empty; independent carrier renamings preserve that interface. Thus a
term token cannot enter a garbage-collection hole. Output closure follows
because there is no term input from which an operation-only path could
reach an output. Raising the allowance preserves the primitive tree and
the two zero-use distributors; it raises the arbitrary payload recursively.
These are the hypotheses of the previously proved nullary-template theorem.
That theorem constructs the actual matching rewrites and original residuals
for all nine local rules. Applying it in both directions gives the claimed
exact contextual equivalence, without a garbage-collection simulation axiom.

\subsection{Ordered contraction algebra}
\termdef{ordered-contraction-algebra}
Fix a sharing sort $s$, either term or atom, and write $c_s$ for the
ordered two-input contraction corolla. Associativity compares the two
three-input trees $((0,1),2)$ and $(0,(1,2))$. Commutativity compares the
literal ordered lists of sources $[1,0]$ and $[0,1]$. The crossing is a real
incidence change: external input positions are fixed by every recursive
graph isomorphism used in the proof. No external permutation is hidden in
an isomorphism of the displayed fragments.

\termdef{sharing-tree-pair}
More generally, a sharing-tree pair consists of two genuine finite acyclic
contraction/weakening trees of the same sort and the same ordered input
length, together with independent recursive graph representations of those
trees. This is a structural predicate. Its definition contains no execution,
simulation or contextual-equivalence hypothesis. Both outputs are blocked
from operation-only paths, since each output has a sharing-edge producer.
The predicate is preserved by reversal and depth padding. The central new
result proves full input safety and both robustness directions for every
padding-closed pre-template whose pairs have this structural shape.

\begin{theorem}[Exact sharing-template equivalence]
Suppose every pair of a padding-closed pre-template is a sharing-tree pair.
Any original recursive paired filling of a closed observing graph preserves
exact successful-transition counts. The same assertion holds at an arbitrary
typed nonrewrite common token, provided an actual endpoint is search-rooted.
In particular, it holds for the literal associativity and commutativity
families, for both sharing sorts, at arbitrary addressed observing sites.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Maximal sharing components}
\termdef{rooted-sharing-basin}
A rooted sharing basin selects finitely many original host edges, a selected
root edge, and its unique exit wire. Every selected edge has exactly one
output and has a finite selected-edge path to the root. The root has no
selected sharing successor. Source and target wires have the selected
sharing sort. The exit may be an internal wire of the host.

Forward adjacency is functional because output lists are singletons and a
wire has a unique consumer. Each selected edge therefore has a unique
terminal route and an exact distance to the root. An adjacency step decreases
that distance by one. This proves acyclicity of the normalized component.
The maximal basin contains \emph{all} sharing predecessors of its root,
including inactive sibling branches. Its boundary consists of precisely the
incident wires with no selected producer, together with the distinguished
exit. A finite distinct boundary enumeration may place one selected input,
or two distinct selected inputs, first, while retaining every other input.

\termdef{sharing-context-expansion}
Normalization turns the selected component into a sharing-tree context.
Its old template holes remain actual holes with their original ordered
interfaces. Restricting the original recursive filling certificates to the
selected edges yields actual filled trees on both sides. Expanded acyclicity
uses the host distance together with each inserted tree's distance, in
lexicographic order. Erasing the allowance changes only primitive labels:
identity carrier maps recover actual contraction trees at the original
allowance. Crucially, the resulting paired-tree witness retains the
\emph{original} pre-template relation at every hole. It does not declare
arbitrary unrelated trees to be an instance of associativity or commutativity.

\termdef{common-context-cut}
A complete occurrence in the original common context determines a finite
complement by removing exactly its matched edges and internal vertices.
Unlike a retained-only footprint, this cut may include shallow template
holes. Expanding the original selected fillings and its complement
reconstructs each original whole graph. Two proved local rewrites therefore
lift through one common cut, preserving the actual token and every untouched
recursive filling. The local residual is grafted into that same context.
This constructs an original quasi-specimen, rather than merely asserting
that a matching residual must exist.

\subsection{All nine operational cases}
For allocation and unary or binary arithmetic, the actual outer footprint
contains nonsharing primitives and the original stable operand. Such an
edge cannot originate in a shallow sharing-tree filling. A retained box
may still contain arbitrarily deep paired changes. Primitive rigidity
reconstructs both local rewrites and their exact common residual.

For beta reduction, the application, lambda and box are retained outer
edges; the box body may contain arbitrary recursive sharing-template
fillings. The original stable argument is unchanged. Padding closure
allows the actual paired bodies to be opened at the required allowance.
The proved substitution/expansion laws construct the paired beta reducts,
without a binding-free restriction on the common context.

For dereference, the selected actual instance determines the first input
of a complete maximal atom-sharing basin. Its root exits into the actual
store. The common cut includes the literal reader, instance and store
corollas and all original sharing holes in that basin. On each side the
selected expansion normalizes to the original dereference rule. The rule
retains the store and requests the prescribed copying of its contents.

Assignment uses the same maximal atom component and the actual unchanged
stable value. Stable labels exclude sharing edges; the complete value
therefore reflects into the original context with all its output ports.
This preserves the original stability premise. The constructed residual
uses the actual assignment rule and retains every inactive atom input.

True reference equality selects two distinct ordered instance entries in
one maximal atom component. Both entries remain first in the normalized
boundary. False equality selects two components ending at distinct original
stores. They are disjoint: a common sharing predecessor would have two
terminal roots, contradicting functional forward adjacency and uniqueness
of the consuming store. Both actual equality outcomes thus have explicit
paired original rewrites and original recursive residuals.

Copying uses a maximal \emph{term} component. Search rootedness gives
operation-only reachability of the selected original token, so that token
has no sharing producer and is an actual component input. The original
copyable payload has no outer weakening or contraction; its lambda body
may still contain arbitrary recursive changes. Its complete retained
skeleton is disjoint from the sharing basin. Joining the two components
therefore yields an actual full copying source. Both original copy rules
fire at its first input. The residual retains the selected input's new
weakening, the remaining entire tree, both separately certified payload
copies and the literal term and atom output distributors.

These cases exhaust the original local-rule datatype. Every arbitrary
contextual rewrite supplies its own complete local occurrence. Proved
successor coherence identifies the constructed result with any separately
supplied representation-class successor. Reversing the original filling
certificates repeats the construction for every opposite-side rewrite;
reversing the resulting quasi-specimen restores the original orientation.
Thus no case classification, matching rewrite or residual-existence
assumption remains in the universal sharing theorem.

\subsection{Input safety and rooted execution}
\termdef{sharing-entry-safety}
Every exposed term input of a genuine sharing tree is consumed by a
contraction: a weakening has no source. It therefore requests copying in
one actual search transition. At an entered specimen both sides make that
request at the same common host coordinate. If no rewrite follows, the
opposite matching theorem proves that both requests are stuck. Otherwise
both sides perform one actual rewrite and reach an equality-up-to residual.
These are exactly input-safety Cases I and III, with costs one/one or
two/two. Reflexivity of the cost preorder discharges the comparisons.

\termdef{sharing-nonrewrite-root-reflection}
A pure search run ending at a nonrewrite common token cannot have entered
a filled term-sharing hole: that entry would stop at rewrite mode, from
which pure search has no transition. Backtracking out of a sharing hole is
also excluded by operation-only reachability. Reflecting the remaining
steps gives an equally long pure-search run in the literal common context.
Conversely every retained context step expands to a filled search step.
Both actual endpoints therefore have the same original search roots at
nonrewrite common tokens. This root theorem, rather than an inference from
observational equivalence, permits exact corrections from one actual rooted
endpoint. Applying concrete robustness sufficiency gives the exact initial,
pointed and addressed execution consequences stated above.

\subsection{Pulling weakening through a box}
\termdef{literal-box-weakening-pull}
For an arbitrary focus-free body with $n$ bound outputs, adjoin one free
weakening output of sort $s$. On the left this weakening lies inside the
box; on the right it is outside the original box. The selected free output
is inserted between the free term and atom blocks, and all $n$ bound outputs
remain fixed. The fragment's only input is a thunk. Output closure and
input safety follow from the absence of term inputs; they do not by
themselves prove robustness.

The beta diagram is a geometric commutation of adjoining the unused output
with the original bound-output splice. Serial and parallel port maps keep
every argument attachment fixed. Both branches take the actual beta rule
with the same original stable argument, and their successors are recursively
isomorphic. No termination assumption is made on the body or callback.

\termdef{box-weakening-copy-correction}
The copying overlap duplicates the outside weakening and retains the entire
canonical $D_{1,2}$ row. That row itself has a weakening and two contractions.
Consequently the new unused resource contains three weakenings feeding two
contractions. Two genuine Idem witnesses reduce it to the single outside
weakening. The first Idem step alone is insufficient. Complete occurrences
identify the full original row; after replacing it, an edge partition proves
that the intended outside-weakening reduct covers the corrected graph.
The resulting isomorphism fixes its ordered external boundary.

\termdef{box-weakening-residual-chain}
A residual records one original box-weakening paired filling followed by
the two Idem corrections. It does not assert transitivity of the original
box-weakening filling relation. Expansion retains every unrelated original
box-weakening contribution in the first link and uses reflexive Idem
certificates on those other components in the correction links. Exact Idem
semantics are applied only after the original residual specimen; their
intermediate search roots and hole freedom are proved from the actual
endpoints. This yields the precise refinement-up-to clause.

The forward proof normalizes all left contributions to their actual
corollas. An atomic box has exactly three possibilities: retained, deeply
paired, or a shallow literal box-weakening occurrence. These cases cover
beta and every constructor of copyability; the primitive cases are rigid.
Induction through the original rewrite constructors then handles arbitrary
whole graphs and mixed recursive contributions.

The converse reconstructs enabled left rewrites from arbitrary actual
right occurrences. A separate weakening has no input, and every other
primitive contribution is rigid. Selected minimal atom or term paths omit
unused weakening siblings from the local match while retaining them in the
host complement. Lambda reconstruction keeps the original \emph{larger}
free interface and original body, rather than replacing it by the smaller
right box. Allocation, read, assignment, both equality outcomes, arithmetic,
beta and every copyable payload are covered. Forward transport and proved
successor coherence then supply the residual for any given right successor.
This establishes full converse robustness. Concrete sufficiency gives exact
successful counts in every original observing context, including recursive
addressed sites. Semantic weakening and the final Proposition~12.2 chain
remain separate tasks.

\subsection{Checked intermediate work for the remaining families}

resource distributors. Its output closure and padding laws and the
original request at every input are checked; the distinguished first input

are now also proved. The first input takes the original two-versus-zero
prefix. Every other input takes two actual transitions on either side and
uses the genuine three-payload sharing correction. Full copying robustness

The box-contraction pair retains the two selected adjacent free outputs and
the literal contraction inside or outside the box. Its structural laws and
stable beta diamond are checked. The copying interchange has genuine

The whole corrected successor is now reconstructed and the full forward
