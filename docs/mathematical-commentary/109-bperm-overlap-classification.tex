\section{Classifying BPerm overlaps and two operational templates}
\label{sec:copying-overlap-classification}
The new result starts from an arbitrary actual contextual rewrite on the
left side of an original BPerm specimen. It extracts an original local rule
occurrence and classifies its source: a primitive-only footprint with a
constructed matching occurrence on the right, an application head, or a
copying head. The application case has a complete three-edge frame whose
box contribution is retained, shallow, or recursively deep. Copying's
counted sharing paths reflect into the original context with their lengths
unchanged. No overlap classification is assumed as an extra specimen field.

This implements the source analysis needed for the BPerm cases of the paper's
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/1907.01257v5\#A8.SS3.SSS6}{Appendix H.3.6}.
It does not yet construct every right-hand \emph{whole-graph rewrite} or its
residual specimen. In particular, an incidence-preserving match does not
include the surrounding replacement context. This distinction was the remaining obligation at that milestone;
Section~\ref{sec:copying-full-bperm} now discharges it for every case. Separately, the micro-beta and
reference-creation pre-templates from the paper's
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/1907.01257v5\#S12.SS2}{Section 12.2}
now have proved output closure, global focus freedom, and actual compute
steps. Their input safety and robustness remain open. The original terms,
translation, operation rules and stable-argument premises are unchanged.

\subsection{Outer provenance in the original recursive filling}
\termdef{bperm-outer-provenance}
Let $C$ be the original common context and $L,R$ its certified filling
families. Write $C[L]$ and $C[R]$ for their simultaneous expansions.
An outer edge of $C[L]$ is a pair $(e,x)$, where $e$ is an edge of $C$ and
$x$ is an edge of $L_e$. Call $e$ its \emph{owner}. A literal BPerm endpoint
has one outer box edge and no internal vertices; its body is unrestricted.
The property \texttt{NoOuterPrimitive} says that no outer edge has a primitive
label. These three facts persist under independent recursive isomorphisms
on the two endpoints: use the edge bijection, the map into the empty
internal carrier, and preservation of box-versus-primitive labels.

\lean{Copying/BoxPermutationOrigins}{10}{11}{NoOuterPrimitive}
\lean{Copying/BoxPermutationOrigins}{15}{35}{UpToIso.outer_shape}

Now inspect an original \texttt{DeepFillingEdge} certificate. A retained
contribution is its original labelled corolla. A shallow contribution is a
BPerm pair and has the shape just proved. A recursively boxed contribution
is again a single box corolla. Thus every component has at most one edge and
has no internal vertices. If an actual component edge is primitive, the
shallow and boxed cases are impossible: only the retained constructor
remains, giving both original \texttt{LabelCopy} certificates and the exact
primitive label in $C$.

\lean{Copying/BoxPermutationOrigins}{41}{50}{primitive_retained}
\lean{Copying/BoxPermutationOrigins}{53}{58}{component_singleton}
\lean{Copying/BoxPermutationOrigins}{60}{65}{component_no_internal}

The owner projection on actual edges is injective: equal owners place both
edges in a singleton component. Every actual outer vertex is the image of
a vertex of $C$ under the host inclusion; the putative internal-component
case is empty. The final provenance theorem packages the original primitive
label together with both corolla certificates. None of these statements
requires that a particular redex has already been selected.

\lean{Copying/BoxPermutationOrigins}{68}{72}{owner_injective}
\lean{Copying/BoxPermutationOrigins}{76}{84}{vertex_origin}
\lean{Copying/BoxPermutationOrigins}{87}{93}{primitive_origin}

\subsection{Reflecting and reconstructing primitive occurrences}
\termdef{bperm-primitive-footprint}
A \emph{primitive footprint} is an outer graph all of whose edge labels are
primitive. It imposes no restriction on the enclosing host. Define a map
$\pi_L:C[L].V\to C.V$ by undoing the host inclusion on internal host vertices,
inputs and outputs. The remaining vertex case is eliminated by the preceding
empty-carrier result. Both inverse equations hold by cases on vertices.
For every primitive edge $(e,x)$, its ordered source and target lists project
under $\pi_L$ to the corresponding lists of $e$. This follows from its
retained-corolla certificate and the inverse equations, not merely from
unordered adjacency.

\lean{Copying/BPermPrimitiveMatching}{10}{11}{PrimitiveFootprint}
\lean{Copying/BPermPrimitiveMatching}{19}{23}{PrimitiveMatching.down}
\lean{Copying/BPermPrimitiveMatching}{25}{25}{PrimitiveMatching.down_host}
\lean{Copying/BPermPrimitiveMatching}{27}{31}{PrimitiveMatching.host_down}
\lean{Copying/BPermPrimitiveMatching}{33}{42}{PrimitiveMatching.source_down}
\lean{Copying/BPermPrimitiveMatching}{44}{53}{PrimitiveMatching.target_down}

Let $f:A\hookrightarrow C[L]$ be an actual \texttt{MatchEmbedding} with
primitive footprint $A$. Reflect it into $C$ by composing its vertex map
with $\pi_L$ and projecting each edge to its owner. Edge injectivity follows
from injectivity of $f$ and of the owner projection. Primitive labels and
both ordered incidence equations follow from provenance and the list laws
above. Boundary ports are allowed to alias on attachment, just as in the
original occurrence interface.

\lean{Copying/BPermPrimitiveMatching}{59}{71}{PrimitiveMatching.reflect}

For every edge of $A$, provenance supplies a right retained-corolla
certificate. Choose these proved certificates and map that edge to the
chosen edge at the same owner in $C[R]$. Map its vertices by the reflected
occurrence followed by the right host inclusion. This constructs the
opposite occurrence, with every source and target position preserved.
The selected input token maps to the same original host coordinate on both
sides. A footprint edge whose label is not a hole cannot belong to a shallow
replacement: otherwise equality of primitive labels would make it a hole.
Classical choice selects structural certificates already proved to exist;
it assumes no operational or observational conclusion.

\lean{Copying/BPermPrimitiveMatching}{73}{77}{PrimitiveMatching.rightCopy}
\lean{Copying/BPermPrimitiveMatching}{80}{94}{PrimitiveMatching.across}
\lean{Copying/BPermPrimitiveMatching}{96}{102}{PrimitiveMatching.across_host}
\lean{Copying/BPermPrimitiveMatching}{104}{109}{PrimitiveMatching.avoids_shallow}

\subsection{The complete application frame}
\termdef{bperm-box-origin}
For a selected actual box edge, \texttt{BoxOrigin} records one of three
original certificates: an unchanged retained box, a shallow BPerm pair at an
original hole, or a recursively filled original box with its actual related
bodies. The classification is obtained by cases on the existing edge
certificate. A retained primitive label cannot be recursively equivalent to
the selected box. Consequently a shallow case is an original hole and the
other cases are original boxes. In the deep case, focus freedom of the
original body and its recorded recursive filling construct a
\texttt{FillingWitness} for the two actual bodies.

\lean{Copying/BPermOverlapClassification}{11}{22}{BoxOrigin}
\lean{Copying/BPermOverlapClassification}{26}{37}{box_origin}
\lean{Copying/BPermOverlapClassification}{41}{47}{BoxOrigin.hole_or_box}
\lean{Copying/BPermOverlapClassification}{50}{54}{BoxOrigin.deep_witness}

An \texttt{Application.Match} records the application edge, lambda edge,
box edge, their joining wires and the selected body. Its
\texttt{Classification} asserts that the first two edges are retained
primitive labels, records \texttt{BoxOrigin} for the third, and proves that
only the third can have a shallow-hole owner. Apply primitive provenance
to the application and lambda labels; either would contradict the label
of a hole. Distinct frame edges have distinct owners by the previous
injectivity theorem. This is a classification of the frame; the stable
argument and the rest of the host still need their residual assembly.

\lean{Copying/BPermOverlapClassification}{63}{70}{ApplicationOverlap.Classification}
\lean{Copying/BPermOverlapClassification}{71}{84}{ApplicationOverlap.classify}

Given an arbitrary actual rewrite with an application head, the existing
rewrite inversion theorem supplies a complete opening certificate, including
the independently represented successor. Classify its actual matched frame
as above. The conclusion is therefore quantified over real contextual
rewrites, not only over a caller-supplied bare three-edge picture.

\subsection{Sharing paths and the original rewrite head}
If adjacency leaves a primitive edge of $C[L]$, it reflects into $C$ even
when the next edge is a shallow permuted box. Project the common wire;
use the primitive target-list equation and the general source-owner law
for expansion. The same argument reflects a primitive head at its token.

\lean{Copying/BPermPathClassification}{14}{23}{adjacent_origin}
\lean{Copying/BPermPathClassification}{25}{29}{head_origin}

A copying hop leaves a term contraction or weakening. Each of these labels
is retained by provenance, so both its label condition and adjacency
reflect. Mapping an entire inductive trace along the owner projection keeps
its exact number $n$ of hops. A full copying resolution additionally records
its starting source and primitive payload label; these reflect by the same
source-owner and primitive-provenance laws. These are exact \emph{graph-path}
lengths, not newly asserted machine-run costs.

Reflect its head to $C$; inspecting the choice constructors shows that this
head cannot be a hole. Thus every rule family starts at a retained original

\subsection{Exhaustion of the actual rewrite rules}
Primitive corollas have primitive footprints. So do graphs obtained by
lifting an entirely primitive alphabet and stable arguments, since stability
requires a primitive stable label at every outer edge. Expansion preserves
the property componentwise, and serial and tensor composition are its two
specialisations.

\lean{Copying/PrimitiveRuleFootprints}{12}{14}{PrimitiveFootprint.relabel}

Allocation combines a primitive ref head with its original stable argument.
Assignment expands its original pattern with the stable argument, primitive
heads and atom tree. Dereference and both equality patterns are lifts of
primitive-alphabet graphs. Unary arithmetic combines its head and numeric
argument; binary arithmetic combines its head with two numeric arguments.
Each therefore has a proved primitive footprint. The original stability
hypotheses for allocation and assignment are retained.

\subsection{Operation paths under composition}
\termdef{operation-path-composition}
Recall that a graph is one-way when no term output is reachable from an
input by an operation-only path. If every output is an atom or thunk wire,
there is no term output to test. Recursive isomorphisms transport operation
paths by induction on their input and argument constructors, using label and
ordered incidence preservation; applying the inverse proves invariance of
one-wayness.

\lean{Copying/OneWayComposition}{9}{15}{oneWay_of_stable_outputs}
\lean{Copying/OneWayComposition}{19}{26}{Search.OperationReachable.map_iso}
\lean{Copying/OneWayComposition}{31}{34}{OneWay.iso_iff}

If the right factor $H$ of a serial composite is one-way, a path to an
external term output would reflect into $H$ through the existing expansion
path theorem. The composite is therefore one-way, with no restriction on
its left factor. A box is one-way because its sole input is a thunk and an
operation-only path to a term output would require a term input.

\lean{Copying/OneWayComposition}{38}{44}{OneWay.serial_right}
\lean{Copying/OneWayComposition}{47}{53}{OneWay.box}

For arbitrary parallel composition, including typed boundary shuffles,
operation reachability at a vertex of a component reflects into that
component. Induct on the path. An initial external input can belong only to
an input of that component. At an argument step, injectivity of the parallel
vertex map identifies the edge's component with the chosen one. Its local
source list is a singleton; the mapped source equation identifies its root,
and the induction hypothesis reflects the preceding path. Consequently a
term output of the composite lies in a component with a corresponding term
output and reflected path, contradicting that component's one-wayness.
Binary tensor is the two-component case.

\lean{Copying/OneWayComposition}{63}{89}{Search.Parallel.extract}
\lean{Copying/OneWayComposition}{94}{105}{OneWay.parallel}
\lean{Copying/OneWayComposition}{107}{112}{OneWay.tensor}

\subsection{Micro-beta and reference creation}
For micro-beta, write the original source graph as application composed
with the tensor of a closure and stable argument $G$. The closure is lambda
composed with the original body box. Its box is one-way, hence so is the
closure by serial composition. Stability makes every output of $G$ an atom
or thunk, so $G$ is one-way too. Tensor and serial composition prove
one-wayness of the entire beta source. The body can have free term outputs;
this proof is not an assumption that its output list is empty.

\lean{Copying/OperationalTemplates}{13}{20}{Application.before_oneWay}

\termdef{operational-pretemplate}
\texttt{Kind} selects micro-beta or reference creation. The relation
\texttt{Literal} contains precisely the corresponding original before/after
graphs, with the original stable-argument premise and, for beta, focus freedom
of the body. The relation \texttt{Pair} closes these pairs under independently
chosen ordered recursive representations of the two graphs. It does not
change the rule graphs, their interfaces or their evaluation behaviour.

\lean{Copying/OperationalTemplates}{25}{27}{OperationalTemplates.Kind}
\lean{Copying/OperationalTemplates}{30}{36}{OperationalTemplates.Literal}
\lean{Copying/OperationalTemplates}{39}{42}{OperationalTemplates.Pair}

Focus freedom of a beta pair follows from the body and stable-argument label
laws through its original frame and splice. For reference creation, the ref
head, stable argument and allocation prefix introduce no focus label.
The beta source is one-way by the preceding proof. The reference source is
one-way because stability supplies its unchanged output list with no term
wire. Transfer both facts through the endpoint isomorphisms. The
\texttt{template} constructor packages the pair relation and its focus-free
law; \texttt{template\_outputClosed} chooses the proved left one-way endpoint.
This discharges output closure for both concrete operational pre-templates,
without any robustness, simulation or termination premise.

\subsection{Completed obligations and remaining boundary}
The completed source classification is universal over the original
contextual rewrite derivation and original specimen data. Primitive
occurrences, the complete beta frame and exact sharing paths have proved
provenance. The two operational templates have proved output closure.
For full BPerm robustness, the next obligation is to recover compatible
replacement contexts, construct matching right-hand whole-graph rewrites
in the classified cases, and build their residual specimen certificates.
Those constructions must handle stable operands and mixed copying payloads,
then support both universal robustness directions. Source matching alone
cannot discharge them. The other template safety/robustness proofs,
correspondence audits, semantic weakening and Figure~22 remain open.

The full build passes 503 jobs. All 2869 axiom reports pass, including all
89 new declarations: 6 abstract, 494 graph and 2369 copying audits. Only
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound} occur.
Proposition~12.2 is still unproved. The completion estimate remains roughly
60--70\% of mathematical effort, with low confidence. Source classification
removes a concrete obstacle, but the universal residual construction and
remaining template proofs still prevent a defensible higher estimate.
