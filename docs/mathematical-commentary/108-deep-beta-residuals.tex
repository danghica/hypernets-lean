\section{Deep beta residuals and stability of BPerm fillings}
\label{sec:copying-beta-residuals}
This section closes the local deep beta residual-certificate obligation.
An arbitrary recursively certified pair of bodies may be opened by the
original micro-beta rule, preserving a genuine recursive filling certificate
for the exposed bodies. Independently certified arguments are allowed;
stability on the left implies stability on the right. For closed, hole-free
bodies this gives an original quasi-specimen and the forward and converse
robustness conclusions for every independently chosen source successor.
A shallow permuted box can also be combined with a separately certified
stable argument: its actual successors are recursively isomorphic, with
exact successful costs in every typed host from the redex input.

The dependency is relevant to the application-overlap cases in the paper's
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/1907.01257v5\#A8.SS3.SSS6}{Appendix H.3.6}.
These local results and the copying results of
Section~\ref{sec:copying-residual-specimens} are extended by the source-overlap
classification of Section~\ref{sec:copying-overlap-classification}. Matching
whole-graph operation transfers, general residual certificates and universal
forward/converse robustness assembly remain open. Full BPerm
robustness and Proposition~12.2 remain unproved. No syntax, translation clause,
local rewrite rule, stability condition or observation class is changed.

\subsection{Corollas with their actual interfaces}
A \texttt{LabelCopy} certificate describes a graph with one edge, no internal
vertices, and the original ordered boundary ports of a given label.
The input and output lemmas recover its interface from that label's signature:
apply the graph's source/target typing equations to the certified port lists.
This is needed when a recursive box certificate is reconstructed at interfaces
that were not syntactically written as that signature.

\lean{Copying/FillingTransport}{10}{13}{LabelCopy.input}
\lean{Copying/FillingTransport}{15}{18}{LabelCopy.output}

Two such corollas at the same boundary are recursively isomorphic whenever
their labels are recursively equivalent. The internal-vertex map is the unique
map between empty carriers; the edge map takes the unique edge to the unique
edge. The inverse equations use uniqueness, label compatibility uses the
given label equivalence, and each port list is fixed position by position.
Uniform depth padding retains the chosen edge and all its ports, changing
only its label. This constructs the padded \texttt{LabelCopy}.

\lean{Copying/FillingTransport}{21}{36}{LabelCopy.iso}
\lean{Copying/FillingTransport}{39}{45}{LabelCopy.padding}

The constructor \texttt{of\_copies} starts from the reflexive filling of the
left corolla and transports the right endpoint along this isomorphism.
The constructor \texttt{boxedCopies} takes an actual paired body certificate,
boxes it, and transports both resulting corollas to the given representations.
Its interface equalities are derived by the input/output lemmas above.
It introduces neither a new filling constructor nor a semantic equivalence
assumption.

\subsection{Padding the relation and the recursive certificate}
\termdef{bperm-filling-padding}
Write $\uparrow G$ for the original one-level increase of box allowance.
The external output action by a typed permutation $p$ commutes with padding:
\[
 \uparrow(G\cdot p)=(\uparrow G)\cdot p.
\]
After replacing padding by its uniform graph presentation, both sides have
identical carriers and labels. Equality of incidence maps follows by checking
internal vertices, inputs and outputs. Applying this identity to the two
literal sides of a BPerm pair proves closure of the raw relation.
Transporting the two endpoint isomorphisms gives closure up to recursive
representation; focus freedom is preserved by the existing padding law.
Thus the actual BPerm template, not merely a structural derivation relation,
is closed under padding.

\lean{Copying/BoxPermutationPadding}{9}{20}{InterfacePermutation.raise}
\lean{Copying/BoxPermutationPadding}{24}{28}{Step.raise}
\lean{Copying/BoxPermutationPadding}{30}{33}{UpToIso.raise}
\lean{Copying/BoxPermutationPadding}{35}{37}{template_raise}

For an arbitrary pre-template $T$, \texttt{PaddingClosed} denotes precisely
$T(G,H)\Rightarrow T(\uparrow G,\uparrow H)$. The BPerm instance is the theorem
just proved. To lift an entire filling, first reassemble separately padded
component certificates in the padded host. Their label-signature equalities
supply the necessary boundary casts. Padding commutes with simultaneous
expansion up to the previously proved ordered graph isomorphism, so the
result has the intended expanded endpoints.

\lean{Copying/FillingPadding}{9}{10}{PreTemplate.PaddingClosed}
\lean{Copying/FillingPadding}{12}{13}{template_paddingClosed}
\lean{Copying/FillingPadding}{19}{28}{FillingWitness.paddedExpansion}

Induction on the available box depth proves existence of a padded witness.
For a retained edge, use its padded corolla certificates and preserved focus
freedom. For a filled hole, use the proved padding closure of $T$. For a boxed
edge, recurse on its actual inner filling and reconstruct the two represented
box corollas. The last case cannot occur at depth zero. Reassembly and padding
of the original endpoint isomorphisms finish the induction.
\texttt{raise} selects this constructed witness, and \texttt{pad} iterates it
for any natural number of padding levels. Classical choice here selects
proved structural data; it supplies no machine or template law.

\subsection{Stability is preserved, and stable BPerm fillings are rigid}
\termdef{stable-filling-preservation}
First, the property that every outer edge has a stable label is invariant
under recursive isomorphism. For the forward direction, choose the inverse
image of an edge and use preservation of label equivalence; for the reverse
direction use the forward edge map. A pre-template
\texttt{PreservesStableLabels} if each of its pairs preserves this property.
Each literal BPerm side contains a box edge, whereas a stable label must be
an instance edge or a passive primitive operation. Hence neither endpoint
of a BPerm pair up to isomorphism can satisfy this outer-label property.
The implication in either direction is therefore valid, proving the required
preservation law for the actual BPerm template.

\lean{Copying/FillingStability}{9}{16}{stableLabels_iso}
\lean{Copying/FillingStability}{18}{20}{PreTemplate.PreservesStableLabels}
\lean{Copying/FillingStability}{23}{38}{UpToIso.not_stable_labels}
\lean{Copying/FillingStability}{40}{43}{template_preservesStableLabels}

The edge-level filling lemma has three cases. Retained corollas have the same
label. A filled hole uses the pre-template's preservation law. A boxed
contribution has a box on both sides and so cannot have only stable labels.
Quantifying this result over each component edge gives the corresponding
law for simultaneous deep filling. Transport across a witness's endpoint
isomorphisms then gives
\[
 W_T(G,H)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
   \bigl(\operatorname{Stable}(G)\iff\operatorname{Stable}(H)\bigr).
\]
Here $W_T$ abbreviates an actual \texttt{FillingWitness}, and the preservation
law for $T$ is required. The original output-type and reachability clauses of
stability are retained: they follow from the transferred label restriction
by the existing alphabet-specific \texttt{Stable.of\_labels} theorem.

\lean{Copying/FillingStability}{45}{69}{DeepFillingEdge.stable_labels_iff}
\lean{Copying/FillingStability}{71}{80}{DeepFilling.stable_labels_iff}
\lean{Copying/FillingStability}{84}{89}{FillingWitness.stable_iff}

\termdef{stable-bperm-rigidity}
For BPerm there is a stronger structural result:
\[
 W_{\mathrm{BPerm}}(G,H)\ \land\ \operatorname{Stable}(G)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad G\cong_{\mathrm{deep}}H.
\]
To prove it, examine each edge certificate in the witness's common context.
A shallow template filling is impossible by the box obstruction above.
A recursively boxed filling is impossible for the same reason at its outer
edge. Only a retained corolla remains. Both expansions therefore collapse to
the same original context by the existing corolla-collapse isomorphism.
Composing these collapses with the endpoint isomorphisms proves the claim.
This does not identify general BPerm pairs with ordered isomorphisms: the
additional flat-stability hypothesis is essential.

\subsection{Before and after the actual beta rewrite}
\termdef{deep-beta-filling}
Let $B,C$ be depth-$d$ bodies with ordered outputs
$\operatorname{env}(k+1,h)$, and let $G,H$ be depth-$(d+1)$ arguments with one
term input and a common arbitrary output list $O$. Suppose $W_T(B,C)$ and
$W_T(G,H)$ are given. The splice constructor expands the original two-slot
splice pattern with these two certificates. It connects exactly bound output
zero; all remaining body outputs and all argument outputs keep their order.
The source constructor puts a retained application head before the tensor
of the boxed body certificate and the argument certificate. Thus it certifies
the actual graphs \texttt{Application.before}, including their original boxes.

\lean{Copying/BetaFillingWitness}{11}{17}{FillingWitness.splice}
\lean{Copying/BetaFillingWitness}{19}{23}{FillingWitness.betaBefore}

Assume now that $T$ is padding-closed. Opening the body uses exactly the
original increase of depth allowance and
the equality $\operatorname{env}(k+1,h)=\mathsf{term}::\operatorname{env}(k,h)$.
The padding theorem and an interface cast give its certificate. Splicing with
the argument yields
\[
 W_T\bigl(\operatorname{after}(B,G),\operatorname{after}(C,H)\bigr).
\]
Unlike shallow opening, these residual endpoints need not be isomorphic:
they can retain arbitrary nested template occurrences. The certificate uses
the original recursive filling relation and has no termination hypothesis.

\lean{Copying/BetaFillingWitness}{25}{28}{FillingWitness.openBody}
\lean{Copying/BetaFillingWitness}{32}{36}{FillingWitness.betaAfter}

rule independently on both sides: each rewrite token at the redex input
becomes a search token at that input in the corresponding \texttt{after}
graph. The preceding construction certifies the residual pair. Starting with
a search token instead gives the original six-transition search-and-compute

\subsection{Quasi-specimens and the robustness conclusion}
\termdef{deep-beta-robustness-case}
With $k=h=0$ and $O=[]$, assume both bodies hole-free. The definitions
\texttt{source} and \texttt{target} package before/rewrite and after/search
as unbounded states. The step theorem projects literal beta to the deterministic
representation-class machine. The residual certificate gives an original
quasi-specimen up to equality: hole freedom follows from the bodies and stable
arguments, and each target is a rooted initial search state.

\lean{Copying/DeepBetaRobustness}{11}{13}{DeepBeta.source}
\lean{Copying/DeepBetaRobustness}{14}{16}{DeepBeta.target}
\lean{Copying/DeepBetaRobustness}{18}{21}{DeepBeta.step}
\lean{Copying/DeepBetaRobustness}{24}{30}{DeepBeta.quasi}
\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
Exchanging the paired filling gives the converse system's quasi-specimen.

\lean{Copying/DeepBetaRobustness}{32}{36}{DeepBeta.quasi_opposite}

Given any independently chosen successor $u$ of the left source, determinism
identifies it with the constructed left target. For any cost preorders
$Q,P,R$, choose zero further steps from $u$ and one step from the right source.
Then $Q(1+0,1)$ follows from reflexivity, and the residual equality
quasi-specimen lifts to one up to the two refinement preorders. This proves
the complete quantitative robustness conclusion for this source pair.
The converse proof exchanges sides and uses derived stability on the right.
The theorem is universally quantified over bodies, certified arguments and
independent successors; it is still a case theorem, not the whole template's
robustness quantification over arbitrary specimens.

\lean{Copying/DeepBetaRobustness}{40}{49}{DeepBeta.robust_case}

from the two before/search states is equivalent to equality of all successful
costs from the two after/search states. Both whole-host prefixes have the
same length, so subtracting or adding six proves the two implications.
This result records the remaining residual comparison explicitly. It does
not assume or assert general semantic soundness of a BPerm filling.

\subsection{Completed obligation and remaining boundary}
The local deep beta transfer, its genuine residual certificates, both closed
robustness cases, and the shallow beta case with a separately certified
stable argument are now proved. Padding of actual recursive certificates and
stable-filling rigidity are reusable prerequisites. The mixed-host example
also instantiates the transfer with a genuinely independent residual
contribution at another hole.

Section~\ref{sec:copying-overlap-classification} subsequently proves the
source-overlap classification for arbitrary original specimens and actual
rewrites. Full BPerm still needs matching whole-graph rewrites and residual
components for all those classified cases. The remaining operation transfers
and universal assembly still belong to Appendix~B.1; the local results in this
section alone do not discharge them.
The final Proposition~12.2 equivalence chain is not proved.

67 new declarations; only \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and
\texttt{Quot.sound} occur. Estimated completion remains 60--70\% of
mathematical effort, with low confidence. This removes a concrete beta proof
gap, but the difficulty of the remaining universal template arguments still
prevents a defensible narrower or higher estimate.
