\section{Exact local semantic weakening}
\label{sec:local-semantic-weakening}
This milestone begins the remaining semantic weakening argument for
Proposition~12.2. It follows the two-template dependency in the paper's
Proof~12.3 (HTML numbering): Idem removes redundant weakenings and BPullW
moves an unused resource out of a box. The terms and the Figure~16 translation
are unchanged. In particular, the canonical one-use and two-use distributors
are retained before their contextual equivalence is proved.

The completed forest laws hold for every finite number of resources, both
sharing sorts, and every box-depth allowance. The completed boxed law allows
arbitrarily many bound variables but no other free resources. Section~\ref{sec:resource-semantic-weakening} subsequently extends this
boxed calculation to additional free resources and proves the unary and
abstraction induction steps. The complete induction on terms remains open. These local results do not by themselves prove the paper's full
weakening proposition or Proposition~12.2.

\subsection{Finite calculations and their exact meaning}
\termdef{weakening-calculation}
\begin{definition}[Weakening calculation]
A calculation from $G$ to $H$, with fixed ordered input and output interfaces,
is a finite proof tree. A leaf is either an ordered recursive graph
isomorphism with a focus-free source, a genuine Idem filling witness, or a
genuine BPullW filling witness. A tree can reverse a calculation or concatenate
two calculations at an identical intermediate graph. It carries the actual
recursive filling certificates, not an assertion that the template relation
is transitive. The identity calculation uses the identity isomorphism.
\end{definition}
\lean{Copying/WeakeningCalculation}{14}{22}{Weakening.Calculation}
\lean{Copying/WeakeningCalculation}{26}{27}{Weakening.Calculation.refl}

\begin{lemma}[Admissible intermediates]
Both endpoints of a calculation are focus-free. One endpoint is hole-free
if and only if the other is hole-free.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the finite calculation. At a filling leaf, focus freedom is part
of the original witness. An Idem pair has no holes on either side; BPullW
preserves the body's hole labels. The proved recursive filling theorem lifts
these statements to the complete filled graphs. Isomorphisms preserve the
same predicates. Reversal swaps the two conclusions and concatenation
composes them. No invariant for every primitive-label predicate is claimed:
Idem can remove contraction labels.
\end{proof}
\lean{Copying/WeakeningCalculation}{30}{37}{Weakening.Calculation.focusFree}
\lean{Copying/WeakeningCalculation}{41}{49}{Weakening.Calculation.holeFree_iff}

A calculation lifts to an arbitrary addressed site in a focus-free host by
induction on its proof tree. Each leaf is lifted through that same site by
the existing original filling construction, or by replacement congruence
for its isomorphism. Thus the site may be inside any number of boxes.
\lean{Copying/WeakeningCalculation}{52}{60}{Weakening.Calculation.plugAt}

Serial lifting on the right retains a fixed focus-free suffix; serial lifting
on the left retains a fixed focus-free prefix. At a filling leaf, serial
assembly pairs its witness with a reflexive witness for the retained graph.
At an isomorphism leaf, serial congruence gives the corresponding leaf.
For two calculations $G\leadsto G'$ and $H\leadsto H'$, first calculate
$G;H\leadsto G';H$ and then $G';H\leadsto G';H'$. This two-stage construction
allows the two components to use different template families.
\lean{Copying/WeakeningCalculation}{63}{71}{Weakening.Calculation.serialRight}
\lean{Copying/WeakeningCalculation}{74}{82}{Weakening.Calculation.serialLeft}
\lean{Copying/WeakeningCalculation}{85}{88}{Weakening.Calculation.serial}

The tensor constructions have the same proof: retain one component, lift
every leaf in the other, and then compose the two calculations. The
ordered tensor boundary is unchanged. Boxing lifts every Idem or BPullW
witness into the original recursive box and applies box congruence to
isomorphisms. The binder count and all bound-output positions remain fixed.
\lean{Copying/WeakeningCalculation}{90}{98}{Weakening.Calculation.tensorRight}
\lean{Copying/WeakeningCalculation}{100}{108}{Weakening.Calculation.tensorLeft}
\lean{Copying/WeakeningCalculation}{110}{113}{Weakening.Calculation.tensor}
\lean{Copying/WeakeningCalculation}{116}{124}{Weakening.Calculation.boxed}

Interface transport changes only equal interface indices. Raising the depth
allowance transports each original witness using its proved padding-closure
law. Iterating this construction gives finite padding; transporting the
result along $d+(N-d)=N$, for $d\leq N$, gives padding to a chosen allowance.
These are representation operations, not additional machine transitions.

\lean{Copying/WeakeningCalculation}{139}{146}{Weakening.Calculation.raise}

\begin{theorem}[Exact semantic soundness]
If a calculation relates hole-free graphs of interface $*\to\epsilon$, their
initial states have equal-cost refinement in both directions in the original
unbounded copying machine. Consequently they have the same successful runs
at every specified transition count. If the endpoints are open programs,
they are exactly contextually equivalent in every original binding-free
closing context.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the calculation. Idem and BPullW leaves use their completed
robustness consequences. An isomorphism leaf can be expressed as a reflexive
Idem filling transported by that isomorphism. Reversal swaps the two
refinements, while concatenation uses transitivity of machine refinement.
The preceding invariant supplies hole freedom of every intermediate.
To obtain contextual equivalence, first lift the calculation through the
closing context's actual site. Its unique-hole certificate proves that the
resulting graphs are hole-free. To recover the count-by-count statement,
apply refinement to a successful $n$-step run and use its exact cost relation
to identify the returned count with $n$. No termination premise is needed.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The original unary and binary rows}
\termdef{zero-fed-sharing-row}
Write $w_s:\epsilon\to s$ for a weakening of sharing sort $s$, and $C_m$ for
the original canonical $m$-input contraction row. A \emph{zero-fed row} has
a weakening attached to every external input of $C_m$.
The unary graph is $w_s;C_1$. It has two weakening edges and one contraction;
the input-free weakening internal to $C_1$ is still present.
\lean{Copying/WeakeningRows}{12}{13}{Weakening.unaryRow}

\begin{lemma}[Unary and binary absorption]
The graphs $w_s;C_1$ and $(w_s\otimes w_s);C_2$ each calculate to $w_s$,
for either sharing sort and every allowance.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The unary graph is ordered-isomorphic to the literal Idem source. Its
incoming weakening is the second contraction input, and the row's own
weakening is the first. The isomorphism exchanges their carrier tags,
accounts for both internal wires, and fixes the external output. One Idem
witness therefore proves the unary calculation.
For the binary graph, the already established row isomorphism exposes a
nested Idem source with three weakenings and two contractions. First collapse
the non-root Idem occurrence, retaining the root weakening and contraction;
then collapse the root occurrence. These are two actual Idem witnesses,
not a substitution of a lone contraction for the canonical row.
\end{proof}

\lean{Copying/WeakeningRows}{103}{106}{Weakening.unary}
\lean{Copying/WeakeningRows}{110}{113}{Weakening.binary}

Lifting these two calculations to an arbitrary original addressed site and
applying exact soundness proves their unrestricted-host observational laws.
The selected local interface is input-free; these theorems do not invoke a
binding-free plugging lemma that requires a nonempty input interface.

\subsection{Ordered finite forests}
\termdef{weakening-forest-normalization}
Let $W_k=D^s_{k,0}:\epsilon\to s^k$ denote the original zero-use distributor.
The Lean constructor exposes its empty input by an equality transport;
it does not rebuild or simplify the distributor.
\lean{Copying/WeakeningForests}{11}{12}{Weakening.forest}

A finite parallel family of forests $W_{m_a}$ is isomorphic to $W_k$ whenever
its output boundary bijection enumerates exactly $k$ ordered resources.
Both graphs have no internal vertices. Map the weakening at local resource
$(a,j)$ to the weakening at the global output position prescribed by the
boundary bijection, keeping its unique edge index. The inverse uses the
inverse boundary map. This proves both bijection laws and equality of every
ordered incidence, including when the family is empty.
\lean{Copying/WeakeningForests}{16}{60}{Weakening.forest_parallel_iso}

\begin{lemma}[Independent serial composition]
Suppose $F_a:\epsilon\to J_a$ and $G_a:J_a\to O_a$ form a finite family,
with an ordered boundary $B$ assembling the $G_a$. Assembling all $F_a$,
then composing with the assembled $G_a$, is ordered-isomorphic to assembling
the componentwise composites $F_a;G_a$.
\end{lemma}
The prefix boundary uses $B$'s input bijection as its output bijection and
has the unique empty input action. The resulting boundary retains $B$'s
output action. These define the two literal sides of the lemma.
\lean{Copying/NullaryParallelSerial}{15}{20}{NullaryParallelSerial.beforeBoundary}
\lean{Copying/NullaryParallelSerial}{23}{28}{NullaryParallelSerial.afterBoundary}
\lean{Copying/NullaryParallelSerial}{30}{30}{NullaryParallelSerial.left}
\lean{Copying/NullaryParallelSerial}{31}{31}{NullaryParallelSerial.right}

\begin{proof}
A shared wire in global position $j$ maps to the pair consisting of its
unique component and local position, given by $B^{-1}(j)$. Internal vertices
from either component retain their owner and switch only the order of the
component and serial tags. Edges undergo the same tag reassociation.
For each prefix output and suffix input, the two port maps agree by
$B^{-1}B=\mathrm{id}$. Other ports are retained. Mapping each edge's complete
source and target lists proves the ordered graph isomorphism. The proof
allows heterogeneous component interfaces and an empty enumeration.
\end{proof}
\lean{Copying/NullaryParallelSerial}{127}{157}{NullaryParallelSerial.iso}

The original distributor $D^s_{k,m}$ is ordered-isomorphic to its $k$ original
canonical rows at any allowance. This is the commuting of primitive relabelling
with parallel assembly, proved on every vertex and incidence. Combining this
presentation with the preceding two lemmas exposes the exact rowwise form of
$W_{km};D^s_{k,m}$. In particular, its resource order remains $i+kj$ for
resource $i$ at use $j$.
\lean{Copying/WeakeningForests}{64}{87}{Weakening.distributor_rows}
\lean{Copying/WeakeningForests}{92}{103}{Weakening.distributor_feeding}

The one-resource forest $W_1$ is isomorphic to $w_s$: its empty internal
carrier disappears and its single component tag is removed. Likewise,
$W_2$ is isomorphic to the two ordered weakenings $w_s\otimes w_s$; resource
indices $0$ and $1$ select the two tensor components. In both cases the
proof checks every original weakening output.
\lean{Copying/WeakeningDistributorLaws}{10}{40}{Weakening.forest_one_iso}
\lean{Copying/WeakeningDistributorLaws}{48}{83}{Weakening.forest_two_iso}

Using these isomorphisms, the unary row has a single original Idem filling
from $W_1;C_1$ to $W_1$. The binary row has a first filling from $W_2;C_2$
to the literal Idem source and a second filling from that source to $W_1$.
These certificates are obtained by transporting the preceding literal row
witnesses, without weakening their relation or changing their graph sources.
\lean{Copying/WeakeningDistributorLaws}{87}{92}{Weakening.unary_filling}
\lean{Copying/WeakeningDistributorLaws}{95}{99}{Weakening.binary_first_filling}

\begin{theorem}[Finite distributor weakening]
For every $k,d\in\mathbb N$ and $s\in\{*,\diamond\}$, there are checked
weakening calculations
\[
 W_{k\cdot1};D^s_{k,1}\leadsto W_k,
 \qquad W_{k\cdot2};D^s_{k,2}\leadsto W_k.
\]
They preserve every successful-transition count in any original surrounding
closed graph, including at arbitrary box depth.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Expose the rowwise source by the proved ordered isomorphism. For the unary
case, assemble all unary Idem certificates in parallel. For the binary case,
assemble all first certificates, then all second certificates. Finally,
flatten the resulting forest of one-resource weakenings using its actual
output boundary. Exact soundness of calculations supplies the observation
claim. The construction includes $k=0$ without a positivity premise.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Pulling and absorbing a boxed unused resource}
First consider an independent graph $G:I\to\epsilon$, a graph
$W:\epsilon\to J$ and a suffix $R:J\to O$. The literal composites
$(G\otimes W);R$ and $G\otimes(W;R)$ have the same ordered boundary.
Their isomorphism moves the shared $J$-wires into the second tensor
component and reassociates the internal and edge ownership tags. Since $G$
has no outputs, it contributes no shared wire. Its input and all its edges
remain unchanged. Portwise commutation for the three components proves
equality of every incidence list; arbitrary nested boxes in $G$ are retained.

\termdef{boxed-unused-resource-law}
\begin{definition}[Boxed unused-resource pair]
Let $G:*\to *^n$ be any focus-free body with $n$ bound outputs and no free
resources. Let $L$ be the actual BPullW box with one extra unused free
resource of sort $s$ inside it; let $R$ be the actual BPullW target, consisting
of the original box and an outside weakening. The constructors expose the
common interface $T^n(*)\to s$ by equality transport alone.
\end{definition}
\lean{Copying/WeakeningBoxLaw}{13}{14}{Weakening.BoxLaw.inside}
\lean{Copying/WeakeningBoxLaw}{17}{18}{Weakening.BoxLaw.outside}

The outside presentation is ordered-isomorphic to the tensor of the original
closed box and $w_s$. The isomorphism retains both edges and, in particular,
the original recursive body label. The first calculation $L\leadsto R$ is
one actual BPullW witness with its original focus-free-body condition.
\lean{Copying/WeakeningBoxLaw}{22}{39}{Weakening.BoxLaw.outside_iso}

\begin{theorem}[Boxed unary absorption, closed-resource case]
There is a checked calculation $L;C_1\leadsto R$, for arbitrary binder count,
body depth and sharing sort. It preserves exact successful-transition counts
at every original recursively addressed observing site.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply BPullW beneath the unchanged suffix $C_1$. Expose $R$ as the tensor of
the original closed box and $w_s$. The preceding independent-component
isomorphism exposes $w_s;C_1$ beside that box. Apply the unary Idem
calculation to this component and restore the outside presentation. Thus the
proof uses one BPullW leaf and one Idem leaf, with only ordered isomorphisms
between them. Addressed lifting and exact soundness yield the last claim.
There is no assumption that a callback or surrounding graph terminates.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Dependency map and the remaining induction}
The row laws depend on the original canonical-row isomorphisms and full
Idem semantics. Forest normalization additionally uses ordered parallel and
serial isomorphisms. The boxed law depends on these unary results, full
BPullW semantics, and the independent zero-output-component isomorphism.
The calculation interpreter combines the two established exact semantic
consequences; it does not assume a robustness theorem for their union.

All these dependencies are proved. The next application obligations are to
handle a selected unused resource while a box retains other free resources,
to extract that resource through the original distributors and shuffles of
each translation constructor, and to assemble the full term induction.
The Figure~22 chain and arbitrary-environment Proposition~12.2 remain open.
